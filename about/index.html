Hello, I’m Dewashish.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay, working with Prof. Chandra Venkataraman on questions at the intersection of atmospheric science, climate change, air quality, and environmental health.

My research asks how aerosols reshape regional climate and extreme heat, how emissions from different sectors influence population exposure, and how those impacts are distributed across rural and urban communities. These questions require moving between physical processes, large model datasets, population health, and socioeconomic context.

I work with chemistry-coupled atmospheric models, climate-model experiments, satellite and ground observations, reduced-complexity air-quality tools, and geospatial methods. My goal is not only to quantify environmental risk, but also to understand the mechanisms behind it and the interventions that can reduce it.

Why this work matters

India faces overlapping pressures from particulate pollution, rapid development, increasing heat extremes, and unequal access to protective resources. Treating these challenges separately can conceal important interactions. I study the full system—from emissions and atmospheric response to exposure, health, and equity—so that mitigation strategies can account for multiple outcomes at once.

How I work

I value transparent methods, careful uncertainty analysis, collaborative research, and results that can be communicated beyond a specialist audience. I am particularly interested in reproducible workflows that lower the barrier to using complex atmospheric models and Earth-system datasets.

Beyond the model

I have contributed to interdisciplinary collaborations connecting IIT Bombay with Duke University, UC Berkeley, the University of Washington, and CSTEP. I have also supported teaching across aerosol technology, climate systems, risk analysis, and climate policy.

Alongside my academic research, I co-developed ACTIONN, a climate-mitigation framework connecting clean cooking, carbon finance, rural entrepreneurship, and climate-aligned livelihoods. The project won the 2025 ACCESS Award in the Climate Mitigation category.

Current direction

I am completing my doctoral work and exploring postdoctoral and research collaborations in atmospheric modelling, aerosol–climate interactions, compound climate–pollution extremes, environmental health, and integrated policy analysis.
