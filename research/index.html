Research

Following risk across the Earth–human system.

My work connects emissions, atmospheric processes, compound extremes, population exposure, health impacts, and socioeconomic vulnerability.

Core themes

Four connected research questions.

Each theme is a different entry point into the same challenge: understanding how atmospheric change becomes unequal human risk.

Aerosol–climate

How do absorbing aerosols affect extreme heat?

I quantify radiative and meteorological responses to black carbon and other absorbing particles, including changes in temperature maxima, heatwave persistence, and dry versus moist heat stress.

Compound extremes

Where do heat and PM₂.₅ extremes coincide?

I examine the spatial and temporal overlap of dangerous heat and particulate pollution, the processes that produce compound events, and the populations facing elevated joint risk.

Extreme-value analysisGeospatial statisticsExposure
Air quality + health

Which sources drive exposure and mortality?

Using sectoral WRF-Chem sensitivity experiments and health-impact functions, I estimate source-specific PM₂.₅ exposure and attributable mortality across India.

WRF-ChemEmissionsHealth impacts
Environmental justice

How are burdens distributed across people and places?

I integrate rural–urban population patterns and socioeconomic vulnerability to identify disparities that national or city-centred averages can obscure.

Current studies

Work in progress.

Ongoing research extends from physical attribution to policy-relevant health and equity assessment.

Study 01

Absorbing aerosols and temperature extremes

Combining observations, reanalysis, and climate-model simulations to estimate regional aerosol influence on dry and moist heat extremes over India.

Study 02

Source influence on rural and urban PM₂.₅ mortality

Evaluating energy, industry, agriculture, residential, and transport contributions to exposure and mortality, together with socioeconomic vulnerability.

Study 03

The 2015 mega heatwave

Quantifying how aerosol–radiation interactions affected heat stress, exposed population, and land area during a major Indian heatwave.

Study 04

Fast air-quality policy assessment

Supporting India-InMAP with WRF-Chem inputs and evaluation workflows for rapid analysis of emission-control strategies and health benefits.
